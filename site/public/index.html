FAQ

Before you download.

What is the OS file, and where does it come from?

The editors do not synthesise the machine. They run its own operating system, so they need a copy of it: Machinedrum OS 1.63 or Monomachine SFX-60 OS 1.32B, a full 8 MiB image (.bin, or a .zip holding it). It is your machine's software, so we do not include it, supply it or link to it. Some people call it the ROM, and the editor does too.

How do I add the OS file?

Open the editor. A start-up card asks for your firmware: click it, choose the .bin (or a .zip with it) in the file chooser, and the editor checks it and copies it into its ROM folder. On macOS you can also drop the file onto the window; on Windows and Linux use the chooser. It takes 10 to 15 seconds to start. The file stays on your computer. Steps, keys and DAW use are in the user guide.

Which systems does it run on?

macOS 12 or later, on Intel and Apple silicon, as an app, VST3 and AU. It is tested on Apple silicon (an M4 Pro). A Windows x64 build and a Linux x64 build exist, but nobody has tried them yet, so treat them as untested. Windows needs the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime. Details are on the download page.

Where can I get help, report a bug or share an idea?

On the MD + MM Editor Discord: new releases are announced there, and it has channels for bugs, ideas and your videos. Or email us from the contact page.

Does it work with my real machine?

The default is the emulator, which needs no cables. An option called HW MIDI is there to drive a real Machinedrum or Monomachine over MIDI. It is in beta: it has only been tested against the emulator, not on a real machine, so keep a backup of your machine first.

Is it really free?

Yes, free software under the GPL-3. If it is useful to you, you can sponsor the work on GitHub; that is optional and the download is the same.

How do I uninstall it?

Delete the bundles the installer put in place. For the Machinedrum Editor: Machinedrum Editor.app in /Applications, Machinedrum Editor.vst3 in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 and Machinedrum Editor.component in /Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components. For the Monomachine Editor: the same with Monomachine Editor.app, Monomachine Editor.vst3 and Monomachine Editor.component. If you installed for yourself only, look in ~/Applications and ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins. Up to 0.3.1 the plug-ins were called Gearmulator MD and Gearmulator MM: delete any of those left in the same folders. Your ROM and settings stay where they are.

My DAW will not load the AU

macOS may have quarantined the unsigned component. In Terminal run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/Machinedrum Editor.component" (use Monomachine Editor.component for the Monomachine, and ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components if you installed for yourself only), then rescan plug-ins in your DAW.

Known limits
  • Tested on macOS, Apple silicon (M4 Pro). The macOS builds run on Intel and Apple silicon (macOS 12 or later). A Windows x64 build (app and VST3) is available but not tested yet, and so is a Linux x64 build.
  • Not notarised by Apple. On macOS 15 and later the first install is blocked: open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to the message about the installer and choose Open Anyway (then confirm). Right-click → Open no longer works for installers.
  • About half a CPU core per instance.
  • Over MIDI you can send a WAV or AIFF to a slot (when the port can send SysEx), but samples cannot be read back: no waveforms, no audition. Live recording and chains need the emulator.
  • Alpha software: keep backups.
