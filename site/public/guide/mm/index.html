MMMonomachine Editor guide

Version 0.5.1

The chapters follow the Monomachine manual, in its order. Each one says what that part of the machine does and how you do it in the editor. To install the editor and add your ROM, see the user guide.

  1. 1Introduction, philosophy, highlightsWhat the Monomachine is, what the Monomachine Editor adds to it, and how this guide writes about controls.
  2. 2User interface and connectorsThe Monomachine's panel, keyboard and sockets, and where each control is on the editor's page.
  3. 3The LCD user interfaceWhat the fields of the LCD in the top bar show, and where the rest of the machine's screen went.
  4. 4Quick startPick a pattern, play it, play the keyboard, draw notes, record live and lock a value on a step.
  5. 5Monomachine overviewSix synth tracks, the sequencer, the MIDI tracks, and the keyboard modes poly, multi trig and multi map.
  6. 6Synthesis arrangement: kits and machinesThe machine families, the kit that holds six machines and their settings, and how to load, save, copy and clear kits and machines.
  7. 7Track effectsThe amp, filter, equaliser, sample rate reducer and delay that every track has after its machine.
  8. 8Low frequency oscillatorsThe three LFOs of each track: where they point, when they start, their shape, speed and depth.
  9. 9Additional kit settingsAssign performance controls to parameters, set how a track's notes and glides behave, and play all six tracks from one keyboard.
  10. 10The pattern sequencer: pattern operationChoose a pattern, chain patterns, and set the length and speed of the pattern that plays.
  11. 11Composing a patternWrite notes in the piano roll or record them live, and copy, move and clear notes, pages, tracks and patterns.
  12. 12TempoSet the tempo by hand or by tapping, and follow a DAW or an external clock.
  13. 13Parameter locksGive a parameter its own value on single steps, draw the values in the lock lane, and record them while the pattern plays.
  14. 14Advanced sequencer controlChoose which envelopes a trig fires, and write trigs that carry only a pitch or only locks.
  15. 15Additional sequencer featuresArpeggiator, transpose, swing, slide, the mute window and poly mode.
  16. 16The MIDI sequencerWrite notes, velocities and controller values for the six MIDI tracks.
  17. 17The song modeArrange patterns into songs of up to 200 rows, with repeats, transposes, parts, mutes, tempo changes and loops.
  18. 18RoutingSend tracks to the mix buses and outputs, feed effects from other tracks or the audio inputs, and choose the routing mode.
  19. 19Global settingsThe machine's own settings: MIDI channels, sync, routing, the multi map, and the SysEx import and export.
  20. 20Early startup menu, technical informationWhat the machine's startup menu does, and what to do in the editor instead.
