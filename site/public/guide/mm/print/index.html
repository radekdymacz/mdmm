1. Introduction, philosophy, highlightsMonomachine Editor

1

Introduction, philosophy, highlights

MM manual pp.1-1 to 1-3

The Monomachine is a six-track synthesizer and sequencer made by Elektron. Each of its six synth tracks plays one machine, a sound generator of its own kind, and runs it through its own filter, effects and three LFOs. A sequencer with 64 steps per pattern plays the six tracks and six more tracks of MIDI notes.

The Monomachine Editor puts the whole instrument on one web page. It runs the real Monomachine operating system, OS 1.32B, from a firmware image you supply. The page edits the machine and shows what the machine reports. The sound comes from the firmware, not from a copy of it.

The editor runs as a standalone application and as a VST3 or AU plug-in. It has two engines. The emulator runs the firmware on your computer. The HW MIDI engine edits a real Monomachine over MIDI with the same page.

MM manual p.1-2

HIGHLIGHTS

The machine's main features, and where each one is in the editor:

  • Six synth tracks with a sequencer. The Sequence and Sound workspaces edit the six tracks of a kit and their patterns.
  • Six MIDI tracks. The switch in the track rail changes between the synth side and the MIDI side. The MIDI tracks have their own page of settings.
  • Machines. Five synthesis families (SuperWave, SID, DigiPRO, FM+ and VO), the basic GND machines and the FX machines. The machine picker on the Sound workspace lists them by family.
  • FX machines. Through, gate reverb, chorus, dynamics, ring modulator, phaser and flanger process audio from the inputs or from other tracks instead of making sound. Set their input in the Mix workspace and their parameters in the Sound workspace.
  • One delay per track. It is tempo synchronised. Its controls are in the Effects groups of the Sound workspace.
  • Three LFOs per track. They sit at the foot of the Sound workspace.
  • Twelve arpeggiators. One for each synth track and each MIDI track. They are in the Sequence dock under Arp.
  • Real-time control. Every value box on the page is live. A value you drag reaches the machine as a controller message at once.
MM manual p.1-2

DIGIPRO USER WAVEFORMS

The DigiPRO machines of an MKII play 64 waveforms, and the MKII lets you load your own. The editor picks a waveform from 1 to 64 in the wave fields of the DigiPRO machines. Export SysEx… saves the user waveforms in a .syx file: see "DIGIPRO MANAGER" in chapter 19. You cannot send waveforms to the machine, rename or erase them in the editor.

The MKII key in the top bar switches the editor between the MKI and MKII plate. The MKII adds the user waveforms and the DigiPRO double draw and ensemble machines. On the MKI plate the machine picker greys those two machines out and says "MKII only".

MM manual pp.1-1, 1-3

CONVENTIONS

This guide writes controls in a fixed way.

  • Bold names a control on the page: Mix, Effects.
  • [SPACE] and [⌘] name a key of your computer. [⌘] is [CTRL] on Windows and Linux.
  • <PLAY> names a field or light on the LCD at the top of the page.
  • The manual's names for the machine's own keys, such as [FUNCTION] and [TRIG], appear where the editor does it differently, with the editor's way beside them.
Note

The Monomachine has a +Drive option for storing snapshots, digibanks and waveforms. The editor does not emulate it.

2. User interface and connectorsMonomachine Editor

2

User interface and connectors

MM manual pp.1-4 to 1-9

The Monomachine has a front panel of keys and knobs, an LCD, a keyboard on the SFX-6 and a row of sockets at the back. The editor has none of these as pictures. Its page holds the same functions as controls of its own, and this chapter names, for each part of the machine, the control that does its job.

The page has a top bar and a body. The top bar holds the five workspace tabs, the LCD with its two lines, and the setup keys Undo, Redo, Global and MKII. The body shows the track rail on the left and the workspace on the right.

The workspaces are Sequence, Sound, Mix, Perform and Song. Press [1] to [5] to go to one. A sixth workspace, Control, appears only when the plug-in has MIDI mapping on, and it is pressed with [6].

MM manual p.1-4

MASTER VOLUME AND LCD

The machine has one master volume knob. The editor has none: the application or the host sets the output level.

The LCD of the machine is the LCD in the top bar. It holds the fields <TEMPO>, <POSITION>, <PATTERN>, <KIT> and <LOCKS>, the record and play keys, and a second line of fields that changes with the workspace. Chapter 3 describes it.

The machine's own screen is not shown after start-up. What it would show is spread over the page.

MM manual p.1-4

LEVEL AND DATA ENTRY

On the machine the LEVEL knob sets the level of the selected track and the eight DATA ENTRY knobs set the parameters of the page shown. In the editor every parameter is a value box.

  • LEVEL is the LEVEL fader of each track in the Mix workspace.
  • A value box changes when you drag it up or down, or sideways. Each pixel of drag is one step.
  • Turn the mouse wheel over a value box to step it, one step a notch.
  • Click a value box and press the arrow keys to step it ([↑] or [↓]).
  • Double-click a value box to set it to its default.

The machine's accelerated edit, where you press a knob and turn it for larger steps, is a different gesture here. Hold [⇧] while you drag for fine steps, a quarter as fast. Hold [⇧] while you turn the wheel, or press Page Up or Page Down on a focused value, to step by ten.

MM manual p.1-4

DATA PAGE

The machine has seven DATA pages for each track: SYN, AMP, FLT, EFX, LF1, LF2 and LF3. It shows one at a time on its eight knobs. The Sound workspace shows every page of the selected track at once, in groups of a few values. The Sequence workspace has the page tabs SYN, AMP, FLT, EFX, LF1, LF2 and LF3 in the track rail. They choose the parameters the lock lane edits.

Pressing both DATA PAGE keys on the machine opens the multi envelope. In the editor the multi envelope is the first card of the Perform workspace. It works in multi trig mode only: see chapter 5.

MM manual p.1-4

TRACK KEYS

The six TRACK keys select the track you edit.

  • Click a track in the rail at the left of the page.
  • Press [↑] or [↓] to select the track before or after.
  • In the Mix workspace, click the track's node in the routing graph.

To mute a track, click M in its rail row or press [M]. Click S to solo it. Drag across several M keys to mute or unmute each track you cross as the first one became.

To prepare mutes, hold [⇧] and click M on the tracks. Each track shows + or X for the change it will make. The changes apply when you let go of [⇧]. This is the machine's mute window.

  • [⌥] + [M] mutes every track. When none is audible it unmutes every track.
  • [0] unmutes and unsolos every track. The M/S off key at the head of the rail does the same.
  • The Mutes card of the Perform workspace lists all twelve tracks, T1 to T6 and M1 to M6.

A muted track dims in the rail. The selected track is lit.

Note

Mutes act on notes. A sound that already plays rings out.

MM manual p.1-4

TEMPO KEY

The <TEMPO> field of the LCD is the tempo window of the machine. Drag it up or down: a pixel is half a beat per minute, and [⇧] makes it a tenth. With the field focused, the arrow keys step it.

To tap a tempo, press [⇧] + [B]. The editor uses the average of your last taps. On every workspace except Sequence a plain [B] taps too. On Sequence that key switches the piano roll's Draw mode.

Note

In a DAW the host sets the tempo. The field is marked and refuses an edit, with a note that says to change it in the DAW.

MM manual p.1-4

FUNCTION KEY

The machine's [FUNCTION] key gives the other keys a second job. The editor has no such key. The second jobs are separate controls on the page, or they follow a modifier key of your computer. Hold [⌥] and the keys it changes say what they do then: <CLR> becomes the clear of the whole pattern, for example.

MM manual p.1-4

KIT AND SONG KEYS

On the machine [KIT] opens the kit menu. In the editor click the <KIT> field of the LCD to open the kit library. Chapter 6 describes it. The Song workspace ([5]) is the song menu: see chapter 17.

[FUNCTION] + [KIT] opens the global menu. In the editor open it in one of three ways:

  • click Global in the top bar;
  • choose GLOBAL… in the engine menu, the drop-down at the left of the LCD;
  • press the GLOBAL link in the AUDIO / MIDI panel.

The panel holds eight global slots and the Control, Sync and Routing settings. [ESC] closes it.

Master tune is not on the GLOBAL panel yet.

MM manual p.1-5

TRIG KEYS

The sixteen TRIG keys play notes and set trigs. In the editor:

  • The keys [A S D F G H J K L] play the selected synth track, from any workspace. They are the white keys C to D. [W E T Y U O P] are the black keys above them.
  • Press [Z] or [X] to move the octave down or up, from −3 to +3. Press [C] or [V] to step the velocity through 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 127.
  • The on-screen keyboard in the Perform workspace plays notes by click or drag.
  • Clicking in the piano roll of the Sequence workspace plays the pitch.

To set a trig on a step, as the TRIG keys do in grid mode, click the piano roll with Draw on, click a dot in the ENV row, or right-click the step for the step menu.

On the machine, [BANK] + [TRIG] picks a pattern. In the editor click the <PATTERN> field of the LCD for the pattern chooser, or the ‹ and › keys beside it. Chapter 4 describes picking.

MM manual p.1-5

PATTERN AND SONG KEY

[PATTERN/SONG] switches between playing a pattern and playing a song. Click <PLAY> in the second line of the LCD. It reads PAT or SONG, what the machine reports, and each click switches. The Song workspace has the same switch.

[FUNCTION] + [PATTERN/SONG] switches POLY mode. In the editor the Perform workspace has a Poly key in the Keyboard card. Chapter 5 describes poly mode.

MM manual p.1-5

ENTER, EXIT AND ARROW KEYS

The machine's [ENTER/YES] and [EXIT/NO] confirm and cancel. The editor asks in dialogs on the page: answer with the dialog's buttons, and close one with [ESC].

The arrow keys of the machine move the cursor and set the octave of the TRIG keys. In the editor [←] or [→] and [↑] or [↓] step a focused value, and [Z] and [X] set the octave of the note keys.

MM manual p.1-5

BANK KEYS

The machine's BANK GROUP and BANK keys choose the pattern bank. The pattern chooser shows all eight banks A to H at once. Press [A–H] inside it to jump to a bank.

[FUNCTION] + [BANK GROUP] opens the mute window: it is the Mutes card in Perform, and the prepared mutes described above.

The keys that open the secondary windows, ARP, TRANSPOSE, SWING and SLIDE, are as follows.

  • Arp and Transpose are tabs of the dock in the Sequence workspace. The Sound workspace has keys that go to them.
  • Swing is the Swing key under the roll of the Sequence workspace, and the <SWG> field of the LCD's second line.
  • Slide is the Slide key under the roll of the Sequence workspace.
MM manual p.1-5

RECORD, PLAY AND STOP

The record and play keys are at the top right of the LCD.

  • Press [SPACE] or click the play key. The machine plays. Press it again to stop.
  • Click the record key while the machine is stopped to switch grid recording on. Editing steps on the page does not need it.
  • Press [⌥] + [SPACE] to start live recording, the machine's [REC] + [PLAY]. Press it again to stop recording.

Grid recording is not needed to edit steps with the mouse. You cannot record by stepping through the pattern in the editor yet.

On the machine a second press of [PLAY] pauses. In the editor:

  1. Hold [⇧] and click the play key, or press [⇧] + [SPACE]. The machine holds its step. The play key blinks and <POSITION> keeps the step.
  2. Click the play key, or press [⇧] + [SPACE] again. The machine goes on from the step it held.
  3. To end the pause instead, choose Stop in the transport menu. <POSITION> reads --.--.

The transport menu has Play or Stop, Pause or Continue, Live record and Panic, each with its key. Right-click the play key to open it, or click the small ▾ in the key's corner.

On the machine a double press of [STOP] rewinds the pattern and silences the notes. In the editor this is Panic: press [⌘] + [.], or choose Panic in the transport menu. The editor sends All Notes Off on the channel of each track and stops the machine. The notes fade out with their release.

Note

Over a MIDI cable the pause is MIDI Stop and Continue, which the Monomachine takes only with <TRANSPORT> set to ACCEPT (GLOBAL, CONTROL IN). The editor asks to set it the first time.

COPY, CLEAR and PASTE are the three keys under the LCD, <COPY>, <CLR> and <PASTE>, with [⌘] + [C], [DELETE] or [BACKSPACE] and [⌘] + [V]. What they act on depends on the workspace:

WorkspaceThey act on
Sequencethe selected steps, or else the page of steps shown
Soundthe machine of the selected synth track, with all seven DATA pages
Performthe assign tabs of the selected track
Songthe selected song row

[⌥] + [DELETE] or [⌥] + [BACKSPACE] on Sequence clears the whole pattern. Clicking <CLR> with [⌥] held does the same.

MM manual p.1-5

SCALE KEY

The machine's [SCALE] key shows one page of 16 steps. In the editor press Page in the Sequence workspace, or press the square bracket keys, to step through the pages. Four lights show the pages the pattern has and which one is shown.

[FUNCTION] + [SCALE] opens the scale set-up. In the editor its fields are in the LCD's second line on Sequence: <LEN> sets the pattern length and <SPD> the speed. Click a field to step it. Chapter 11 describes both.

MM manual p.1-5

TRIG SELECT KEY

On the machine [TRIG SELECT] chooses which trig track the TRIG keys edit: AMP, FILTER, LFO or ALL. In the editor each step has a row of three dots, A, F and L, in the Sequence workspace. A dot lights for each envelope the step fires. Click a dot to switch it. A step with no dots lit is trigless.

[FUNCTION] + [TRIG SELECT] edits the MIDI tracks. In the editor use the Synth and MIDI keys in the track rail, or the <SEQ> field in the LCD, to change between the six synth tracks and the six MIDI tracks.

MM manual p.1-6

THE KEYBOARD (SFX-6)

The SFX-6 has a keyboard and a joystick. The editor has the same functions on the Perform workspace.

  • The Keyboard card plays notes. It has four modes: Auto track, Multi trig, Multi map and Poly.
  • The Assign card has a joystick pad. Drag the stick. It springs back when you let go. On the machine's tabletop models the pad sends pitch bend, CC 1 and CC 2.
  • Multi trig and Multi map are keys of the card.
  • The Octave stepper moves the on-screen keyboard.

The computer keys play notes too: [A S D F G H J K L] and [W E T Y U O P]. The piano roll shows the pitches the machine plays, so the keyboard LEDs have no counterpart.

MM manual p.1-7

REAR PANEL

Power, the voltage selector and the fuse belong to the hardware. The editor has no counterpart.

MIDI sockets

The standalone editor has the AUDIO / MIDI panel for its MIDI. Press [,] or choose AUDIO/MIDI… in the engine menu. The MIDI card lists the inputs, with a light for each that is on, and an output. A button pairs a Bluetooth MIDI device. No MIDI input is on until you switch it on there.

In a plug-in the host routes MIDI. The machine's own MIDI out, which carries notes, controllers, program changes, pitch bend, pressure and clock, reaches the plug-in's MIDI output. Its SysEx never does.

The editor has no MIDI THRU.

Audio inputs and outputs

The audio the host gives the plug-in goes to the machine's inputs. In the Mix workspace each FX machine has an In selector: NEIBOR, INP A, INP B, INP AB or a bus. This is where you choose which input the machine reads.

Each track has Out keys AB, CD and EF. The routing keys under the strips set the global routing: 3xSTEREO, 3xSTEREO + AB = MIX or 6xMONO. A multi-output VST3 host receives the extra pairs. Other hosts receive the main pair.

The headphone output has no counterpart. It is a copy of the AB pair.

MM manual pp.1-8, 1-9

CONNECTING

Mounting, cabling, cleaning and the battery are matters for the hardware. The editor needs only the firmware image, which it asks for on the first start.

3. The LCD user interfaceMonomachine Editor

3

The LCD user interface

MM manual p.1-10

The machine's screen shows the tempo, a level bar, eight parameter circles, four position boxes, the kit and the pattern. The editor's LCD is a strip in the top bar. It shows the same state: the fields that matter all the time stay in view, and the parameters of the screen live in the workspaces.

The LCD has two lines. The first line, from the left: the engine menu, the record and play keys, <TEMPO>, <POSITION>, <PATTERN>, <KIT> and <LOCKS>. The second line changes with the workspace. At its right end it has the sync field, then the keys <COPY>, <CLR> and <PASTE>.

MM manual pp.1-4, 1-10

LCD

Tempo

<TEMPO> shows the tempo in beats per minute, with one decimal. Drag it up or down to change it. When the host sets the tempo, the field is marked as the DAW's tempo and refuses edits. This is the editor's counterpart of the EXT the machine shows when it follows an external clock.

Level and parameters

The machine's level bar is the LEVEL fader of the Mix workspace. The eight parameter circles with their names are the value boxes of the Sound workspace. Each box has the parameter's name and its value.

Position

<POSITION> shows the playback position as bar and step, for example 02.05. It reads --.-- when the machine is stopped. In the Sequence workspace, four lights beside the Page key show which pages the pattern has and the page shown, and a playhead runs across the steps.

Status

The record key and the play key light while the machine records and plays. The play key reads a triangle when stopped and a square when playing. While the machine is paused the play key blinks and <POSITION> keeps the step the pause holds.

Kit

<KIT> shows the number and the name of the current kit, for example K01 MONOMACHINE. Beside the label a small field reads saved or edited. It reads edited when the values of the kit differ from the kit saved in its slot on the machine. Click the field to open the kit library.

Pattern

<PATTERN> shows the current pattern, A01 to H16. The ‹ and › keys beside it step to the pattern before or after. Click the name to open the pattern chooser. A pattern that waits for the end of the current one blinks.

Machine

The machine of the selected track is named in its row of the track rail and in the header of the Sound workspace. The header is also the machine picker: click it to change the machine.

Locks

<LOCKS> counts the locked parameters in the pattern, out of 62. The Monomachine shares those 62 between all twelve tracks. The field warns when you near the limit.

Sync

The sync field at the right of the second line reads Sync when the editor and the machine agree. It reads RECV while the emulator takes a dump from the editor. With a real machine it reads SEND and a number when edits wait to go to it. Click it to send them.

MM manual p.1-10

LAYER EDIT AND WINDOWS

On the machine, windows open over the screen and the keys below them stay live. In the editor, dialogs open over the page with their own buttons. The kit library, the pattern chooser, the machine picker and the GLOBAL and AUDIO / MIDI panels are windows of the page. Press [ESC] to close a dialog or panel.

4. Quick startMonomachine Editor

4

Quick start

MM manual pp.1-11 to 1-14

This chapter takes you through the first things you do with the machine: choose a pattern, hear it, change it and record into it. Each part gives the steps in the editor and says where they differ from the Monomachine's panel. The editor must be running with the firmware image loaded, and the LCD must show the kit and the pattern.

MM manual p.1-11

SELECTING AND PLAYING

A pattern recalls the kit it was saved with. When you pick a pattern, the machine loads its kit.

  1. Click the <PATTERN> field in the LCD. The pattern chooser opens: 8 banks, A to H, of 16 patterns.
  2. Click a pattern. Stopped, the machine switches at once. Playing, it queues the pattern and starts it when the current pattern ends. The name in the LCD blinks while it waits.
  3. To switch at once while the machine plays, hold [⇧] and click, or click Now.
  4. Press [SPACE]. The machine plays and the <POSITION> field counts.
  5. Press [⇧] + [SPACE] to pause, and again to go on. Press [SPACE] to stop.

You can also step through patterns with the ‹ and › keys beside the name.

Note

The OS loads the kit of a pattern only for the patterns in banks A to D. In banks E to H the kit that played stays. When you pick an A to D pattern with kit edits that are not saved, the editor asks first and offers to save the kit, to switch and let the machine keep the edits in its UNDO KIT, or to cancel.

Press [SPACE] again to stop.

MM manual p.1-12

PLAYING IN MULTI TRIG

In multi trig mode one source plays all six tracks. To use it:

  1. Go to the Perform workspace ([4]).
  2. In the Keyboard card, click Multi trig.
  3. Click or drag across the on-screen keyboard. Every track plays the note. The lamps below the keyboard flash for each track that sounds.

Multi trig takes its notes on MIDI channel 7. Chapter 5 describes the other keyboard modes.

MM manual p.1-12

EXPLORING A PATTERN

Play the pattern and listen to each track.

  1. Click a track in the rail to select it.
  2. Press [M] to mute it, or S in its rail row to solo it.
  3. Go to the Sound workspace ([2]) and drag the values of the track. The sound changes as you drag.
  4. Play the track from your keyboard with [A S D F G H J K L].

The piano roll plays the pitch of a note when you click it.

MM manual pp.1-12, 1-13

GRID RECORDING

In grid recording you place notes on the steps of a pattern one by one. The machine does this with the TRIG keys. In the editor you draw in the piano roll of the Sequence workspace.

  1. Go to the Sequence workspace ([1]) and select a track.
  2. If you want the pattern's own kit, open the kit library and load it: click <KIT>, choose a kit, click Load. Chapter 6 describes the library.
  3. Check that Draw is lit. Press [B] to switch it.
  4. Click an empty step in the roll. A note goes on it, as long as the Len key says.
  5. Drag the new note up or down to change its pitch.

To paint the same note on several steps, drag sideways. The steps you cross get the note. A drag is one undo step.

To place a note off, which stops the sound at a step, hold [⌥] and click an empty step. You can also drag the end of a note: the note's length is the distance to its NOTE OFF.

For a chord note on a step, hold [⇧] and click the roll there. Right-click a step for the step menu (Ctrl-click on a Mac). It offers a note on or off, NOTE OFF, trigless, slide and a chord note.

MM manual p.1-13

LIVE RECORDING

In live recording you play the keys while the pattern runs, and the machine writes the notes to the steps that play. The firmware quantises them.

  1. Select the track to record.
  2. Press [⌥] + [SPACE]. The machine starts if it was stopped, and the record light comes on. Clicking the record key while the machine plays does the same.
  3. Play with [A S D F G H J K L] or the on-screen keyboard.
  4. Press [⌥] + [SPACE] again to stop recording.

You cannot erase notes while recording, as the machine does with [EXIT]. Clear steps in the roll instead.

MM manual pp.1-13, 1-14

PARAMETER LOCKS

A parameter lock gives one parameter a value of its own for one step.

Lock a value

  1. In the Sequence workspace, click a page tab in the rail, for example FLT.
  2. Click the parameter, for example BASE. The lock lane under the roll now edits it.
  3. Drag over the bars of the lane. Each step with a trig gets a lock with the value where you dragged.
  4. To draw a straight line of values, hold [⇧] and drag.
  5. To set a locked value from the keys, turn the wheel over a lock step. Each notch is 4, and [⇧] makes it 1.

The <LOCKS> field of the LCD counts the locks of the pattern, out of 62.

Lock live

While live recording plays, turn a value on the Sound workspace. The machine records the value as a lock on the step that plays when the value arrives. The editor marks that step in the trig row and in the lock lane, for about three seconds, and a note says which value it locked.

Remove locks

  • Hold [⌥] and drag over the lane to erase the locks of one parameter.
  • The clear key in the rail header clears the locks of the parameter you are looking at. Hold [⌥] and click it to clear every lock of the track.
  • Select a step and press [DELETE] or [BACKSPACE]. The step loses its note, its slide and its locks together.

You cannot remove the locks of a step and keep its note yet.

5. Monomachine overviewMonomachine Editor

5

Monomachine overview

MM manual pp.1-15, 1-16

The Monomachine has six synth tracks. Each track has its own machine, filter, amp, effects, delay and three LFOs. The sequencer plays the six tracks from one pattern, and six MIDI tracks beside them. The keyboard can play the tracks one at a time, all together, or as a trigger for patterns.

MM manual p.1-15

SIX INDIVIDUAL TRACKS

The six tracks are independent. A kit holds a machine for each and a full set of parameters. In the editor:

  • The track rail at the left lists the tracks T1 to T6 with their machines.
  • The Sound workspace edits the selected track.
  • The Mix workspace shows the six tracks side by side, each with its own level, pan and routing.

Chapter 6 describes the machines and kits.

MM manual p.1-15

SIX TRACK SEQUENCING

A pattern holds the notes, the envelope trigs and the parameter locks of the six tracks. The Sequence workspace edits them: a piano roll for notes, a row of A, F and L dots for the envelope trigs, and a lock lane for the parameters. Chapters 10 to 15 describe the sequencer.

MM manual p.1-15

MIDI SEQUENCING

Six more tracks in each pattern send MIDI notes. Click MIDI in the track rail of the Sequence workspace to edit them. The rail then lists M1 to M6, each with its MIDI channel. Synthesis arrangement: kits and machines

MM manual pp.1-17 to 1-25

Each of the six synth tracks plays one machine. A machine is a kind of sound generator. Its output goes through the same chain on every track: the amp, the filter, the effects and the delay. A kit stores the machines of all six tracks and everything that belongs to them.

MM manual p.1-17

ARRANGEMENT

The machines come in families:

FamilyWhat it is
SWAVESuperWave: analogue style oscillators with unison and sub oscillators
SIDthe 6581 chip, with ring modulation and sync
DPRODigiPRO: digital waves, a beat box of drum sounds, and on the MKII a double draw and an ensemble
FM+frequency modulation, in three arrangements
VOa voice with vowels and consonants

Every track also has the same track effects after its machine: an amp with an envelope and distortion, a filter with its own envelope, an equaliser, sample rate reduction and a tempo synchronised delay. Chapter 7 describes them.

MM manual p.1-18

KITS

A kit holds the machine of each track and all seven DATA pages of each track, with the LFOs. It also holds the MIDI pages of the six MIDI tracks, the routing and input of each track, the trig settings, the multi envelope and the name.

The editor edits the kit the machine plays. It does not write a value to a stored kit slot until you save.

MM manual p.1-18

LINK KITS AND PATTERNS

Every pattern recalls a kit. When you pick a pattern, the machine loads its kit, unless the kit is already the one that plays.

The pattern chooser shows, on each pattern, its length and its kit as K and a number. The kit library shows on each kit slot the patterns that link to it. A kit no pattern links to ends its name with a star and reads "no pattern".

If you pick a pattern with a different kit while the current kit has edits that are not saved, the editor asks. It offers Save kit, then switch, a switch that leaves the edits to the machine's UNDO KIT, and Cancel.

Loading a kit from the library makes it the kit of the current pattern. You cannot set the kit of another pattern directly.

MM manual p.1-19

LOADING A KIT

The machine holds 128 kits. The kit library lists them all.

  1. Click the <KIT> field in the LCD. The kit library opens with the 128 slots as a grid.
  2. Click a slot, or move to it with the arrow keys and press [ENTER]. The kit loads. If the current kit has unsaved edits, the editor asks first.
  3. To select a slot without loading it, hold [⌥] and click.

The current kit is marked in the grid, with ok or ed to say whether it is saved or edited. Slots with a name show it; empty slots read EMPTY. [ESC] closes the library.

Load an empty kit

Load an empty slot to start from nothing. The kit has six tracks with the machine GND-SIN, the sine, and default settings.

MM manual pp.1-19, 1-20

The small field beside the <KIT> label reads edited while the kit differs from its saved slot on the machine, and saved when it matches. The kit edits are kept in the project of your DAW as well.

  • Save writes the current kit to its own slot. The slot's old content goes to the machine's UNDO KIT.
  • Save as writes it to the slot you selected, after asking if that slot is used, and the kit becomes that slot's kit. The slot you saved from keeps what it had.

To rename a kit, select its slot and press [F2], or double-click the slot, or click Rename. Type a name of up to 11 characters and press [ENTER]. The editor takes the name as text, so there is no character picker.

MM manual p.1-20

UNDO KIT

The machine keeps one extra kit, the UNDO KIT, as the first entry of its kit list. It holds the kit lost to the last load or overwrite without saving. The dialogs of the editor name it when an action puts a kit there. The editor's own undo, [⌘] + [Z], works on top of it.

You cannot open the UNDO KIT slot from the editor yet.

MM manual p.1-21

  2. Click Copy, or press [⌘] + [C / V].
  3. Select the slot to copy into and click Paste.

With a real Monomachine the field reads SEND and a number until you send the edit.

To hear a kit pasted into the slot that plays, load that slot again.

  2. Click Clear, or press [DELETE].

The slot gets the empty kit: six sine machines and default settings. The machine has no clear message, so the editor writes the empty kit as a dump, through SYSEX RECV. [⌘] + [Z] undoes a paste, clear or rename.

  2. Go to the Sound workspace ([2]).

PARAMETER EDITING

A track has seven pages of eight parameters, 56 in all. The Sound workspace shows them as groups of a few values, with a small picture where the values draw a shape:

  • the amp, filter and effects pages, the same on every machine;

7. Track effectsMonomachine Editor

7

Track effects

MM manual pp.1-26 to 1-34

The sound of a machine passes through the same chain on every track: amp, filter, equaliser, sample rate reduction and delay. The chain is the same for every machine, so these parameters mean the same on every track. All of them are on the Sound workspace, in the groups under the machine's own. Select the track, press [2] and drag the values.

A value box changes when you drag it, turn the wheel over it or press an arrow key on it. Double-click it for its default. Where a group has a picture, drag the dots in the picture to change the values they stand for.

MM manual p.1-26

LEVEL

LEVEL is the track's mix level, at the end of its path. It is the LEVEL fader of the track's strip in the Mix workspace. It can neither be locked on a step nor moved by an LFO. To balance tracks against each other use LEVEL. For movement inside the sound use VOL.

MM manual pp.1-26, 1-27

AMP ENVELOPE

The Envelope group of the amp page holds four values.

ValueWhat it sets
ATKthe attack: how long the level takes to rise after a trig
HOLDhow long the level stays up, before the decay starts
DECthe decay: how long the level takes to fall
RELthe release: how long it falls after a NOTE OFF

ATK, DEC and REL are times. Each 8 more about doubles the time. ATK 64 reaches 90 percent of the level in about 0.14 seconds, and DEC 64 falls 20 dB in about 0.55 seconds. DEC 127 holds the level until the NOTE OFF, and REL 127 never fades. HOLD counts in steps of the pattern: HOLD 8 is one step, so it follows the tempo.

The picture shows the shape as the machine plays it, with the release dashed. Drag its dots to set the values.

Tip

The Trig setup plate in the Sequence workspace draws the same envelope together with the portamento, and shows how long a gate really sounds in steps at the current tempo.

MM manual p.1-27

DISTORTION

DIST is in the Drive group of the amp page. Drag the dot in its picture up or down, or use the value box. Above 0 it overloads the sound. Below 0 it adds headroom: the sound is quieter and cleaner.

MM manual p.1-28

TRACK VOLUME

VOL is the volume of the voice. It is in the Level · pan group of the amp page, and it is the trim at the top of each strip in the Mix workspace, as AMP VOL. VOL comes before LEVEL in the path. Unlike LEVEL you can lock it on a step and an LFO can move it.

MM manual p.1-29

PAN

PAN places the track in the stereo field, from L64 to R63. It is in the Level · pan group and in each strip of the Mix workspace, where you drag it sideways. Double-click it for the centre.

MM manual p.1-29

PORTAMENTO

PORT sets how long the pitch glides from the note before to the next note. It is in the Glide group of the amp page and in the Trig setup plate of the Sequence workspace. In the plate, Port chooses when it glides: ALWAYS glides to every note, and LEGATO glides only between notes that overlap.

MM manual p.1-30

FILTER

The filter of each track is a 24 dB filter built from a high-pass and a low-pass. The Filter group of the filter page holds four values:

ValueWhat it sets
BASEthe cutoff of the high-pass
WDTHthe gap from the high-pass up to the low-pass
HPQthe resonance of the high-pass
LPQthe resonance of the low-pass

In the picture, drag the dots sideways for BASE and WDTH and up for HPQ and LPQ. A dashed line shows where the filter envelope takes the filter.

MM manual p.1-31

FILTER ENVELOPE

The Filter env group holds ATK and DEC, the attack and decay times of the filter envelope, and BOFS and WOFS, how far the envelope moves BASE and WDTH.

MM manual p.1-31

FILTER TRACKING

Both filters can follow the pitch of the notes. The switches are on the Perform workspace, in the Assign card.

  1. Select the track with the numbered keys in the title of the Assign card.
  2. Click the KEY tab.
  3. Click HPF tracks keys or LPF tracks keys. A lit key means the filter follows the keys.
MM manual p.1-32

EQ

The EQ group holds EQF, the frequency of the equaliser band, and EQG, its gain. A negative EQG cuts the band and a positive one boosts it.

MM manual p.1-32

SAMPLE RATE REDUCTION

SRR, in its own group, reduces the sample rate of the track for a lo-fi sound. Drag its dot, or the value box.

MM manual p.1-33

DELAY

Each track has its own delay, synchronised to the tempo. The Delay group holds three values and the Delay filter group two.

ValueWhat it sets
DSNDthe send into the delay. A negative value keeps the stereo image, a positive value is ping-pong, 0 is no delay
DTIMthe delay time, in 256th notes
DFBthe feedback. The editor warns above 64
DBASthe base of the filter in the feedback path
DWIDthe width of that filter

DSND is also in the trim row at the top of each strip in the Mix workspace, with VOL and DIST.

8. Low frequency oscillatorsMonomachine Editor

8

Low frequency oscillators

MM manual pp.1-35 to 1-37

Each synth track has three LFOs. An LFO moves one parameter of the track, or of the pitch, in a repeating shape. The three LFOs of a track are independent, and an LFO can move a parameter of another LFO.

The LFOs are the bottom row of the Sound workspace. Each has its own group, titled LFO 1, LFO 2 and LFO 3. A group holds, from the top:

  • a picture of the LFO over two bars, with ticks where the LFO is triggered;
  • the target: PAGE and DEST selects, with the TRIG keys beside them;
  • eleven keys for the shape;
  • four value boxes: MULT, SPD, INTL and DPTH.

The group is marked off while its depth is 0.

MM manual p.1-35

LFO

An LFO needs a target. Its target is a page and a destination on that page.

Choose a target

  1. Select the track and go to the Sound workspace ([2]).
  2. In the LFO's group, open the PAGE select. It lists PTCH, SYN, AMP, FLT, EFX, LF1, LF2, LF3 and MIDI.
  3. Open the DEST select and choose the parameter. It lists the parameters of the page. For PTCH it lists eight ranges: 1/12, 2/12, 7/12, 1OCT, 2OCT, 4OCT, 8OCT and 16OCT. For LF1 to LF3 the parameters are those of the other LFOs. For MIDI they are the parameters of the MIDI page.
  4. Set DPTH, the depth, above 0.

Changing the page sets the destination back to the first entry.

Route by dragging

The key marked ~ and the LFO's number, at the head of the group, is a handle. Drag it onto any value box on the page. The value boxes that can be a target are marked while you drag. Drop the handle on one. The editor sets PAGE and DEST to that value, and gives the depth a start value of 32 if it was 0. A note names the LFO and the value it now moves.

MM manual p.1-35

TRIG

The TRIG keys set how the LFO starts.

KeyWhat the LFO does
FREEruns on without being restarted
TRIGstarts again on each trig
HOLDholds the value it has when a trig comes
ONEplays one cycle after a trig
HALFplays half a cycle after a trig

Which steps trigger the LFO is set in the sequencer: see "LFO trigs" below.

MM manual p.1-35

WAVE

Eleven keys, each with a small picture, choose the shape. They are, in order: TRI, ITRI, SAW, ISAW, SQR, ISQR, EXP, IEXP, RMP, IRMP and RND. Click a key to choose a shape. The picture of the LFO redraws.

MM manual p.1-35

MULT

MULT multiplies the speed. Its values are 1X, 2X, 4X, 8X, 16X, 32X and 64X.

MM manual p.1-36

SPD

SPD sets the speed of the LFO. MULT multiplies it. Drag the value box.

MM manual p.1-36

INTL

INTL is the interlace of the LFO. Drag the value box.

MM manual p.1-36

DPTH

DPTH is the depth: how far the LFO moves its target. At 0 the LFO does nothing and its group reads off. Drag the value box.

MM manual p.1-37

LAYERING

Two LFOs can move the same target, and their movements add. A value box that an LFO moves shows a small badge, ~ and the number of the LFO, for example ~1 or ~12 when two LFOs move it. Hover over the badge to read which LFOs these are.

MM manual p.1-37

LFO TRIGS

The sequencer triggers the LFOs on its own. In the Sequence workspace each step has a row of three dots, A, F and L. The L dot of a step triggers the LFOs of the track at that step. It matters for the TRIG, HOLD, ONE and HALF keys. Click the dot to switch it.

MM manual p.1-35

SHARED LFOS

A MIDI track shares its three LFOs with the synth track of the same number. M1 shares with T1, and so on. An LFO that has PAGE set to MIDI moves the MIDI page.

On a MIDI track, the Sound workspace shows the MIDI page, and in the place of the LFO groups a note says which synth track they belong to. Click Open T1 LFOs (with the number of the track) to go to them.

Additional kit settings

MM manual pp.1-38 to 1-44

Besides the sound of each track, a kit holds settings for how the tracks are played: what the joystick, the velocity and the key position control, which track a trig also plays, how the portamento and legato behave, and how a single keyboard drives several tracks. The assign settings and the multi trig, multi map and multi envelope settings are on the Perform workspace. Press [4], or click the Perform tab. The trig track settings are on Sequence.

MM manual pp.1-38, 1-39, 1-40

ASSIGN

Assign lets a performance control move a parameter of the track. Each control has two rows. A row names a page, a parameter on that page, and an amount to add.

The Assign card follows the track selected in the rail. Its header shows the track and its machine, for example Assign · T2 SWAVE-1. The keys 1 to 6 in the header choose another track.

  1. Press [4].
  2. Click a tab to choose the control: JOY RL, JOY U, JOY D, VEL or KEY.
  3. In a row, choose the page: SYN, AMP, FLT, EFX, LF1, LF2, LF3 or PTCH.
  4. Choose the destination. The list holds the parameters of that page for the machine on the track.
  5. Set ADD, from −64 to +63, by dragging the box up or down. Double-click it to return to 0.

Copy, clear and paste an assign

The LCD keys Copy, Clr and Paste act on all the assign tabs of the track while Perform is open.

  1. Click Copy, or press [⌘] + [C].
  2. Choose another track in the Assign header.
  3. Click Paste, or press [⌘] + [V]. The track takes all the tabs of the copy.

Clr empties the tabs of the track. [⌘] + [Z] takes back a paste or a clear.

JOYSTICK

The tabs JOY RL, JOY U and JOY D hold the joystick's three directions: right and left together, up and down. Beside the rows the card shows a stick. Drag it with the pointer to try the assignment. It springs back to the middle when you let go.

MIRR is on the JOY RL tab. When it is lit, a move to the left acts as the same move to the right with the sign reversed. When it is dark, only the right half of the movement acts.

VELOCITY

The VEL tab assigns the velocity of the notes you play to a parameter. The sequencer always plays its notes at velocity 100, so a velocity assign only responds to the keys.

KEY TRACKING

The KEY tab assigns the key you played last to a parameter. The value is low for the lowest key and near its maximum for the highest.

Two keys on this tab, HPF tracks keys and LPF tracks keys, switch the key tracking of the high-pass and low-pass filters.

MM manual pp.1-40, 1-41

TRIG TRACK SETTINGS

These settings belong to one track. They sit in the Trig setup tab, in the header of the lock lane on Sequence. A MIDI track has the MIDI page tab in its place (see chapter 16).

  1. Press [1].
  2. Select a synth track in the rail.
  3. Click Trig setup above the lock lane. The keys of the tab appear in the rail.

Trig position chooses a second track that also plays this track's notes. The keys are T1 to T6, without the track itself, and a dash for none. The Mix workspace draws the link as a dotted cord labelled TRIG and the target track.

Port sets how the portamento works.

  • ALWAYS: the track glides to every note.
  • LEGATO: the track glides only between notes that overlap.

Legato has three keys, AMP, FLT and LFO. A lit key means that envelope fires again when a note starts while the one before still sounds. With all three lit every note retriggers, which suits a bass. With all three dark the notes join smoothly, which suits a lead.

The value boxes ATK, HOLD, DEC and PORT above the picture are the track's amp envelope and glide amount. The picture draws one gate and the glide between two notes. Drag its dots to change the values.

MM manual pp.1-41, 1-42, 1-43

MULTI TRIG

In multi trig mode one source plays all six tracks, as a single large mono synth.

  1. Press [4].
  2. In the Keyboard bar, click Multi trig.
  3. Click one of the four modes: ALL TRK, SPLIT KEY, SEQ START or SEQ TRNSP.
  4. Play the keyboard on the page, or send notes on the multi trig channel. The second line of the LCD shows the channel in the CH field. You set the channel in GLOBAL (chapter 19).

ALL TRK

Every note plays on all six tracks. Each track keeps its own arpeggiator.

SPLIT KEY

The keyboard splits in two. Keys from the split point up play the tracks from Tn to T6. Keys below play T1 to the track before Tn.

  1. Click SPLIT KEY.
  2. Click − or + beside Upper from to choose Tn.
  3. Drag the split marker on the keyboard to choose the split key. The marker reads, for example, SPLIT C-4 · T3-6 above.

In split mode the multi envelope does not act.

SEQ START

Each note you play restarts the pattern. The pattern is transposed by the distance of the note from C-4.

SEQ TRNSP

The first note you play starts the pattern. Later notes only transpose it, so the loop never breaks.

Timing

SEQ START and SEQ TRNSP show a Timing stepper. Click − or + to choose DIRECT, 1/16, 2/16, 4/16, 8/16, 16/16 or 32/16. With DIRECT the note acts at once. With another value it acts at the next division of that length, and the page says so when you play.

MM manual pp.1-43, 1-44

MULTI ENV

The Multi envelope card holds an ADSR that acts on top of every track's own envelope. It acts in multi trig mode.

The five boxes are ATK, DEC, SUS, REL and PORT. PORT is the portamento for all tracks. The plot above them shows the envelope. Drag its dots to change ATK, DEC with SUS, and REL. A REL of 127 never fades, and the plot says so.

Tip

For no effect from the multi envelope, set ATK to 0, and DEC, SUS and REL to 127.

10. The pattern sequencer: pattern operationMonomachine Editor

10

The pattern sequencer: pattern operation

MM manual pp.1-45 to 1-47

The sequencer plays patterns. The Monomachine holds 128 patterns, A01 to H16, in eight banks of 16. A pattern has twelve tracks: six synth tracks and six MIDI tracks. It is up to 64 steps long. The editor shows the patterns on the Sequence workspace. Press [1], or click the Sequence tab.

MM manual pp.1-45, 1-46, 1-47

BASIC PATTERN OPERATION

PATTERN SELECTION

The pattern field of the LCD shows the pattern that plays. Click ‹ or › beside it to go to the pattern before or after. For any other pattern, open the pattern chooser.

  1. Click the pattern field, or focus it and press [ENTER]. The chooser opens.
  2. Click a bank letter, A to H, or press the letter. The row of 16 slots shows that bank.
  3. Click a slot. Each slot shows the pattern name, its length and its kit, for example 32 · K01. A slot with no data reads EMPTY.

While the machine is stopped, a click switches to the pattern at once. While it plays, the machine switches at the end of the pattern that plays. The slot is marked as queued, and the pattern field of the LCD blinks with the name of the pattern that waits. The button in the row above the slots reads Go when the machine is stopped and Queue when it plays.

To switch at once while the machine plays, hold [⇧] and click the slot, or click Now.

Note

A pattern is linked to a kit and loads it. On the OS 1.32B, only a pattern in banks A to D loads its linked kit. In banks E to H the kit that played goes on.

Press [ESC] to close the chooser.

PATTERN CHAINING

A chain plays patterns of one bank one after the other, each once, and then starts again. You build a chain on the Song workspace, on the same pattern palette that builds a song.

  1. Press [5].
  2. Click CHAIN over the pattern palette.
  3. Click a bank, A to H.
  4. Click the pads in the order to play. Each pad shows its number in the chain, up to 16.

The machine plays the chain as soon as there are two pads. If it plays, the chain starts at the end of the pattern that plays. If it is stopped, [SPACE] starts it at the first pad. The line over the bank letters reads CHAIN and the patterns in order, for example CHAIN A03»A05.

  • Click a numbered pad to take it out of the chain.
  • Back takes out the last pad.
  • Clear ends the chain.

Choosing a pattern from the pattern field or the chooser also ends the chain. Editing the patterns of a chain does not.

Note

A chain holds the patterns of one bank. Choosing another bank starts the list again.

Note

In song mode the machine plays the song, not the chain. Click PATTERN or CHAIN over the palette to leave song mode.

SCALE SETUP

The scale of a pattern is its length and its speed. Both are on the second line of the LCD while Sequence is open.

#### Length

LEN sets the length of the pattern, from 2 to 64 steps. All tracks share it.

  • Click LEN for the next full page: 16, 32, 48, 64 and then 16 again.
  • Hold [⇧] and click for the page before.
  • Scroll the wheel over it to change the length one step at a time.

The key beside it, ×2, doubles the pattern. The new half is a copy of the first half, with every track's steps, slides, swing steps and locks. A pattern of more than 32 steps cannot double, and the page says so. [⌘] + [Z] takes the doubling back in one step.

A page is 16 steps. The Page key on the bar above the lock lane steps through the pages. The keys [ and ] go to the page before and after. The four lights beside Page show how many pages the pattern has and which one you see. All shows every step at once. Fol makes the page follow the play position.

#### Speed

SPD sets the tempo multiplier of the pattern: 1X, 2X, 3/4X or 3/2X. Click it for the next value, or hold [⇧] and click for the one before.

#### Song rows

A song row can play a part of a pattern with an offset and a length of its own. See "Play part of a pattern" in chapter 17.

11

TEMPO

The tempo is global. A pattern does not store it, so changing the pattern never changes the tempo. A song row can carry a tempo of its own (see chapter 17, "Set a tempo for a row").

The Tempo field of the LCD shows the tempo in BPM with one decimal. It is on every workspace.

MM manual p.1-56

THE TEMPO SCREEN

You set the tempo in the Tempo field itself, from 30 to 300 BPM, or in the TEMPO window that a click on the field opens.

  • Drag the field up or down. Each pixel is half a BPM. Hold [⇧] to change it a tenth of a BPM at a time.
  • Click the field and press [↑] or [↓] to change it by 1 BPM. Hold [⇧] for a tenth.

The decimal part is kept, so 120.5 is a tempo.

Click the field without dragging to open the TEMPO window, the machine's tempo screen:

  • − and + change the tempo by 1 BPM, or a tenth with [⇧].
  • TAP taps the tempo.
  • NUDGE shifts the tempo for a moment, as [LEFT] and [RIGHT] on the machine. Press and hold −10 % or +10 %. The tempo is 10 % slower or faster while you hold it, and goes back when you let go. The keys [-] or [=] do the same while held.
  • Change on release holds a dragged tempo back until you let go of the field, as [FUNCTION] held does on the machine. The field shows the new tempo while you drag and the machine takes it at the release.
  • Sync INT and EXT choose the machine's own tempo or MIDI clock, as <TEMPO IN> in GLOBAL. Sync in GLOBAL opens the GLOBAL panel.

Press [Esc] or click outside the window to close it.

Tip

Hold [⌥] while you drag the field to change the tempo at the release once, without the tick.

Note

In a DAW the machine follows the tempo of the DAW and the field refuses edits. The page says "The DAW sets the tempo. Change it there."

TAP TEMPO

  1. Press [⇧] + [B] in time with the music. It works on every workspace.
  2. After the second tap the tempo is set and the page shows it, for example Tap tempo: 128.0 BPM.

On workspaces other than Sequence, [B] taps too. On Sequence [B] is the Draw key of the piano roll.

The machine runs on its own clock or follows a MIDI clock. The setting is in the GLOBAL panel.

  1. Click Global in the top bar.
  2. In the Sync card, click EXT beside Tempo in to follow a MIDI clock, or INT for the machine's own tempo.
  3. Click ON beside Ctrl in if the machine should also follow MIDI start and stop.

A parameter lock gives one parameter a value of its own on one step. The parameter takes the value when that step plays and goes back to the kit value after it. A lock needs a trig: a step without one cannot hold a lock.

TRIG TRACKS

Every note on a synth track is a trig. A trig can fire the amplitude envelope (AMP), the filter envelope (FILTER), the LFO envelope (LFO), or any of them together. The machine keeps one trig track for each. The editor shows them in the ENV row under the piano roll, with the label A F L.   • A note you draw in the roll fires all three envelopes.
  • Click a dot to switch that envelope off or on for the trig.
  • Drag across the dots of a row to switch a run of steps the same way.
  • A single trig has one dot lit: AMP only, FILTER only or LFO only.

The editor shows the trig colours as dots, one colour for each envelope. It does not show the machine's trig key lights.

A trigless trig fires no envelope. It is there to carry a pitch or locks.

  • Switch all three dots of a step off, or
  • Right-click the step and choose Trigless (no envelope trigs). Choose it again to put the dots back.

The roll draws a trigless note as an outlined bar. The step menu works on a selection as well.

MM manual p.1-59

A pitchless trig fires envelopes without a pitch of its own. The ENV row shows its dots, and the roll draws it as a dashed bar.

MM manual pp.1-61, 1-62, 1-63

THE ARPEGGIATOR

Each of the twelve tracks has its own arpeggiator, six synth tracks and six MIDI tracks. A pattern holds all twelve. The arpeggiator turns the chord notes of a trig into a run of notes.

  1. Select a track and click Arp.
  2. Write a chord: hold [⇧] and click the steps at the other pitches in the roll.
  3. Set the mode and the other values below.

The light notes in the roll show what the arpeggiator plays.

ARPEGGIATOR BASIC SETTINGS

  • SPD is the speed. 6 is a sixteenth note.
  • RNGE is the number of octaves the run covers.
  • OJMP is the octave jump.

Mode keys:

  • OFF: the track plays the base note of each trig.
  • KEY: the arpeggiator runs while keys are held and plays only the held notes.
  • SID: it needs two or more notes. They stay in the cycle after you release them, until a new chord.
  • ADD: it starts on the first note. Each extra key joins while one key is held.

Play keys: TRUE plays the notes in the order of the chord, UP from low to high, DOWN from high to low, CYCLE up and then down, and RND in a random order. With RND the roll draws the notes outlined, because the pitches vary.

To copy, paste or clear the arpeggiator of a track, tick Arp in Copy…, Paste… or Clear… of the LCD's ▾ menu (chapter 11, "Super copy"). A clear turns it off. Press [⌘] + [Z] to take each back.

The Trig keys AMP, FLT and LFO choose which envelopes the arpeggiator's notes fire again. A MIDI track has no envelopes, so the keys read "none on MIDI".

ARPEGGIATOR RHYTHM AND OFFSET TRACK

Under the values is a strip of 16 cells. It repeats while the arpeggiator runs.

  • Click a cell to mute it or to switch it on again.
  • Drag a cell up or down to give its note an offset from minus 24 to plus 24 semitones.
  • Click a cell past the end, or a key of the LEN row under the strip, to set the length of the strip from 1 to 16.

Transpose changes the pitch of the notes while they play. The programmed notes stay as they are. Click Transpose.

  1. Set TRACK, the transpose of the selected track, by dragging the box. The value runs from −64 to +63 semitones.
  2. Set PAT · all, the transpose of the whole pattern. All tracks follow it.
  3. Under Scale choose ---, FIX, MAJ or MIN.

The line above the keyboard picture says what the track plays, for example plays +5 semitones.

  • ---: no scale. The transposes of the track, the pattern, the song and the multi trig add up. Any note can sound.
  • FIX: only the track transpose acts. Use it for drums and effects.
  • MAJ and MIN: transposed notes stay in the major or minor scale of the key. Keys outside the scale are hatched, in the picture and in the roll.

For MAJ and MIN, click a key of the keyboard picture to set the root key. With --- and FIX, a click on a key sets the track transpose so that C plays that key, upward from C. Hold [⌥] and click to go down to it. The note also sounds while you hold the button.

The song mode plays patterns in a set order. Each row of a song names a pattern and how it plays: how many times, transposed or not, the whole pattern or a part of it, with some tracks muted, at a tempo of its own. Command rows loop back, jump forward or halt the song. Use a song for a finished arrangement, or for a set of loops to play live.

The Monomachine holds 24 songs. A song has up to 200 rows.

In the editor the song mode is the Song workspace. Press [5], or click the Song tab. The Songs column on the left holds the 24 song slots. The middle column holds the PATTERN, CHAIN and SONG keys over the pattern palette, and in song mode the Selected row. On the right, the Arrangement shows every row of the song as a cell, 16 cells to a line.

The machine plays the song. The editor writes the song into the machine and shows what the machine reports: the mode, the row that plays and the tempo.

The three keys over the palette say what the machine plays and what a click on a pad does:

  • PATTERN: the machine is in pattern mode. A pad click goes to that pattern.
  • CHAIN: the machine is in pattern mode and a pad click adds the pattern to its chain.
  • SONG: the machine is in song mode. A pad click adds a row to the song.

To put the machine in song mode:

  1. Click SONG.
  2. The key lights when the machine reports song mode. The line over the bank letters reads the song number and its row count, for example SONG 01 · 65 rows · stopped.

On the machine you press [PATTERN/SONG]. In the editor you can also click <PLAY> on the second line of the LCD, on every workspace: it reads PAT in pattern mode and SONG in song mode, and each click switches.

MM manual p.1-73

LOAD SONG

Loading a song makes it the song the machine plays in song mode, and the song the Arrangement shows. Its slot is lit in the Songs column, and the chip at the top of the column reads its number and name.

  1. Stop the machine. Press [SPACE] if it plays.
  2. Click a slot in the Songs column. The slot is selected. The machine still holds its song.
  3. Click Load, or press [ENTER]. The machine loads the song, and its slot is lit.

Each slot shows its number, its name and its rows. An empty slot reads EMPTY and is drawn faint. Press the arrow keys to move the selection while a slot has the focus.

Note

The machine loads a song only while it is stopped. While it plays, Load is greyed out.

Note

MM manual p.1-74

SAVE SONG

The editor writes each edit into the slot of the song the machine holds as you make it. Save is the machine's SAVE SONG: the machine stores the song it holds into that slot, with its name.

Save a song

  1. Click Save.
  2. If the machine has not loaded your latest edits yet, the editor asks first. Click Load the edits, then save to store the song as you see it.

Save a song into another slot, with a name

  1. Select an empty slot, or a slot to write over, in the Songs column.
  2. Click Save to…. The slot shows a name field with the name of the machine's song.
  3. Type a name, up to 14 characters, and press [RETURN]. Press [ESC] to cancel.

The slot now holds the song, with the new name. The machine still holds the song it had.

Name a song

  1. Select the slot.
  2. Click Rename, press [F2], or double-click the slot.
  3. Type the name and press [RETURN].

Names are in capitals. The editor keeps to letters, digits, spaces and a few signs.

Copy, paste and clear a slot

  1. Select a slot and click Copy, or press [⌘] + [C].
  2. Select another slot and click Paste, or press [⌘] + [V]. The slot holds a copy of the song, with its name.

Note

A slot is written as a song dump, which the Monomachine takes only on SYSEX RECV. With a real Monomachine over MIDI the write waits: the sync field of the LCD reads SEND and the number of writes that wait.

Note

A song stores which patterns it plays, not the patterns themselves. Change a pattern and every row that uses it plays the changed pattern.

MM manual p.1-75

SONG PLAY

In song mode the machine plays the song from row 001, row by row, to its END row.

  1. Click SONG over the palette, or click <PLAY> on the LCD until it reads SONG.
  2. Press [SPACE]. The song plays. The Arrangement header reads Playing and the arrangement is framed.
  3. Read what plays on the line over the bank letters: SONG 01 · row 017 of 65 · B01 means song 01, row 017 of 65 rows, pattern B01. The pattern field of the LCD reads the song and the row in the same way.
  4. Press [SPACE] again to stop.

You can go to pattern mode and back while the song plays. Click PATTERN: the machine plays a pattern and holds its place in the song. Click SONG and the song goes on from that place.

On the machine [PLAY] pauses a song that plays, and [STOP] stops it. In the editor hold [⇧] and click the play key, or press [⇧] + [SPACE]: the song holds its row and step, and the play key blinks. Click the play key to go on from there. [SPACE] stops.

MM manual p.1-75

SONG TRANSPORT

On the machine you move the song pointer to another row while the song plays, and the song goes on from there when the current row ends. In the editor you start the song from the row instead:

  1. Click the row's cell in the Arrangement.
  2. Click ▶ From here in the Selected row card, or press [ENTER].

The machine goes to SONG mode if it was in pattern mode and plays the song from the first step of that row. The editor sends the machine a MIDI song position, the steps of the rows before it, and then Continue.

Note

The Monomachine takes a song position only with <TRANSPORT> set to ACCEPT (GLOBAL, CONTROL IN; the factory setting is IGNORE). The first time, the editor asks to set it, then plays from the row. A loop, jump or halt row before the chosen row cannot be counted through: the editor says so.

The editor shows where the song is. While the song plays, the Arrangement marks the row that plays and the bar above the grid fills up to it. The mark comes from the machine itself, so it follows repeats, loops and jumps.

Note

A song plays to its END row and stops. A loop set to play forever never reaches the end.

Note

A row with a tempo of its own sets the tempo of the machine when it plays.

Note

The machine sends and follows MIDI song position by itself. The editor does not change that.

  • Press [←] or [→] for the row before or after.
  • Click a bar in the time bar above the grid. Each bar is as long as its row plays.

Add patterns

  1. Click SONG over the pattern palette.
  2. Click a bank, A to H. The pads show its 16 patterns, each with its length, or "empty".
  3. Click a pad. A new row with that pattern goes in after the selected row, and becomes the selected row.

Play part of a pattern

  2. Under Part, set Offset, the first step that plays, and Length, the number of steps. The bar above shows the steps that play.
  3. To play the whole pattern again, click Whole pattern.
Tip

Copy, paste and undo

  2. Select the row to paste after.
  3. Press [⌘] + [V], or click <PASTE>. The copy goes in after the selected row.

Note

MM manual p.1-78

SONG LOOPS

A loop row sends the song back to an earlier row, a set number of times. Loops keep a song short and its shape clear. They also make parts longer than the 64 steps of one pattern.

On the machine a loop is a value of the PAT column, after END. In the editor a loop is a command row of its own.

  1. Select the last row of the part to repeat.
  2. Click Add loop in the Selected row header. A loop row goes in after it. Its cell reads ↺ and the row it goes back to, with ×2 under it.
  3. Click − or + beside Back to to set the first row of the part.
  4. Click − or + beside Times to set how many times the part plays, 1 to 63. Click Forever for a loop that never ends. The cell shows ∞.
Note

Loops can sit inside other loops.

Note

In this version, a loop written by the editor goes back to the first row of the song on the machine, whatever row Back to names. This is a known fault.

SONG JUMP

A jump row sends the song forward to a later row. The rows between are not played.

On the machine a loop becomes a jump when you set its row after the loop. In the editor you choose the command.

  1. Select a command row, or add one with Add loop.
  2. Under Command, click JUMP.
  3. Click − or + beside Jump to to set the row to go to. The cell reads → and that row.

On the machine a loop becomes a halt when it points at itself. In the editor you choose the command.

  2. Under Command, click HALT. The cell reads HALT.

MM manual p.1-81

THE MIXER

Each strip holds, from the top: the sound's trim values (AMP VOL, DIST, DSND), PAN, the LEVEL fader, the buses the track sends to, the input, and the M and S keys.

Tracks add into a bus in track order. An effect on a bus therefore hears only the tracks before it.

MM manual p.1-81

An effect machine needs an input and a trig to work. When you choose an effect machine for a track on Sound, the editor sets its input and sets the amplitude envelope values DEC and REL to 127 so that the sound passes. The page tells you so. A trig on the track then opens the effect.

An effect track has an In menu on its strip. The audio inputs are INP A, INP B and INP AB. The drawing shows a dashed line from the input jack to the track.

Note

A synth track has no input. Its strip reads "synth · none".

MM manual p.1-83

NEIBOR ROUTING

NEIBOR is an input that takes the sound of the track before. The drawing shows an arrow from the neighbour. Effects can follow each other this way. Track 1 has no neighbour, so its menu has no NEIBOR.

MM manual p.1-84

MIX BUS ROUTING

BUS AB, BUS CD and BUS EF are inputs that take what a bus carries. If the effect track also sends to the same bus, it replaces what was there: the line below the drawing reads "INSERT". If it sends elsewhere, it takes a copy of the bus: a send. The line names the track that "takes a copy".

ROUTING MODES

Click a mode on the Routing bar. The field ROUTE on the second line of the LCD steps through them on a click. The Routing card of the GLOBAL panel has the same keys.

  • 3xSTEREO: the three buses go to the three stereo outputs, AB to A/B, CD to C/D and EF to E/F. This is the default.
  • 3xSTEREO + AB=MIX: as 3xSTEREO, and CD and EF are also added to A/B.
  • 6xMONO: each track goes to its own output, A to F. The buses are not sent out.

MM manual pp.1-89, 1-90, 1-91, 1-92, 1-93

CONTROL

MIDI CHANNELS

The Control card sets the channels the machine listens on.

  • Base channel: OFF, or 1 to 16. Track t listens on the base channel plus t.
  • Channel span: how many tracks, counting from T1, have a channel of their own. 6 gives all of them.
  • Auto track: notes on this channel play the selected track.
  • Multi trig: notes on this channel play the tracks as the multi trig mode says (chapter 9).
  • Multi map: notes on this channel start patterns as the multi map says.

Click − or + to change a value. The base channel reads as a range, for example 1 to 6, when the span is more than one.

A track has a channel only if its channel is 15 or lower. A base channel of 16 reaches no track. When a track has no channel of its own, the card says which tracks, and that mutes and notes cannot reach them. The editor then sends their sound values as kit dumps.

MORE ON THE MONOMACHINE MIDI FUNCTIONALITY

The machine takes NRPN control and uses program change to select patterns. Automation and MIDI from a DAW reach it as they reach the machine.

CONTROL OUT1

You cannot send the sequencer, the arpeggiator or the keyboard to the MIDI out of the machine in the editor yet.

CONTROL OUT2

The Sync card holds two switches for what the machine sends. Tempo out sends the MIDI clock. Ctrl out sends MIDI start and stop. The Control card has Prg change out: choosing a pattern then sends a program change on the base channel.

CONTROL IN

  • Tempo in: INT uses the machine's own tempo. EXT follows the MIDI clock.
  • Ctrl in: ON makes the machine react to MIDI start, stop and continue.
  • Prg change in: reads BASE. The machine always takes a program change on its base channel, so you cannot change it.

In a DAW the plug-in sets Tempo in to EXT and Ctrl in to ON itself.
